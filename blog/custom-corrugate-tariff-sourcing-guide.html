Custom Corrugate Tariff Sourcing: China vs Thailand and Vietnam

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • November 2026

Why Country of Origin Now Drives Corrugate Cost

Custom corrugated plastic is a low-margin, high-volume material. A 4mm twin-wall PP sheet might sell for a dollar or two per square foot, so a 25 percent duty adds twenty-five cents to every square foot before freight is even quoted. On a 200,000 square foot annual program, that is fifty thousand dollars of pure tariff exposure — money that never touches the product, the tooling, or the freight lane. Procurement teams that still evaluate suppliers on quoted unit price alone are effectively comparing different currencies without knowing the exchange rate.

The discipline that fixes this is landed cost modeling: total delivered cost per square foot, including duty, freight, brokerage, and the working capital tied up in longer transit. Once a buyer models it that way, the sourcing decision usually flips.

How the Duty Stack Works on PP Corrugate

Most polypropylene corrugated sheet and fabricated boxes enter the United States under HTS heading 3923.10, which covers boxes, cases, crates, and similar articles of plastics. The base Column 1 General rate is modest. The complication is what stacks on top of it:

  • Section 301 duties. A large share of Chinese-origin plastic packaging was placed on the Section 301 lists and carries an additional 25 percent ad valorem.
  • Anti-dumping and countervailing duties. Where an AD/CVD order covers a product and country combination, the cash deposit rate can be punitive and is collected at the time of entry.
  • Merchandise Processing Fee. Ad valorem, with minimum and maximum caps, applied to the entered value.
  • Freight and carrying cost. Ocean transit from Asia is longer than domestic trucking, and the inventory carrying cost is real even though it never appears on a customs invoice.

The practical effect is that two suppliers quoting an identical sheet specification can differ by thirty percent at the loading dock.

The Thailand and Vietnam Position

Thailand and Vietnam have become the primary alternative origins for US buyers of PP corrugated because the Section 301 lists are country-specific — they attach to Chinese-origin goods, not to the material itself. As of this writing, no anti-dumping order applies to PP corrugated sheet from either country, so the landed duty position is materially cleaner than China's. That does not mean zero duty. The base HTS rate still applies, and MPF still applies. It means the 25 percent Section 301 layer is absent.

Direct relationships matter here. Working with a manufacturer that owns its extrusion lines in Thailand or Vietnam, rather than a trading company sourcing from multiple origins, keeps the country-of-origin documentation clean and the specification stable across reorders. Elipacko operates on that model — US-available stock plus direct factory production in Thailand and Vietnam, with custom corrugated plastic sheets from Elipacko cut to the buyer's drawing rather than to a catalog size.

Building a Landed Cost Model

A defensible model has five lines per candidate supplier, and each one needs a number rather than an assumption:

  • FOB unit price per square foot at the specified gauge and flute profile.
  • Duty at the correct HTS classification for the finished article, not the raw sheet.
  • Freight to the receiving DC, including drayage and chassis.
  • Inventory carrying cost at your cost of capital, scaled to actual transit time.
  • Quality cost — the rework and line-stoppage exposure when a shipment arrives off tolerance.

Run the model at three volumes. Tariff exposure scales linearly, but freight breaks and minimum order quantities do not, so the ranking can change between a container program and a pallet program.

Documentation That Protects the Duty Position

Country of origin is a legal claim, and US Customs can challenge it. Keep a manufacturer's affidavit on file, retain production records showing where the sheet was extruded and converted, and confirm that the certificate of origin matches the invoiced article. A supplier that cannot produce those documents on request is a duty risk regardless of price. This is also where custom PP packaging boxes programs benefit from a single-origin factory: the paperwork chain is one link instead of four.

Qualifying the Supplier, Not Just the Quote

Ask for the extrusion location, the resin grade and FDA status, the thickness tolerance the line actually holds, and the lead time on a repeat order. A tariff-advantaged origin with unstable quality is a worse deal than a taxed origin that ships consistently. The strongest programs pair a clean duty position with a documented specification and a supplier willing to sign off on it.
