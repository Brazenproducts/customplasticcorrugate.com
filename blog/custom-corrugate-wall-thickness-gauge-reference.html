Custom Plastic Corrugate: Wall Thickness and Gauge Reference

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • October 2026

Specifying custom plastic corrugate without a clear gauge reference is the fastest way to over-pay or under-build a package. Polypropylene twin-wall board is sold by basis weight (grams per square meter) and overall caliper (millimeter wall thickness), and those two numbers drive stiffness, load rating, and unit cost more than any other variable. For procurement and packaging engineering teams, getting the gauge right the first time eliminates re-tooling and avoids field failures that only surface after the first full truckload ships.

How PP Corrugate Is Measured

Unlike fiber corrugated, which is rated by flute profile and edge crush, PP twin-wall is defined by three linked numbers: overall thickness, basis weight, and liner construction. Overall thickness typically runs from 2 mm up to 6 mm for packaging applications. Basis weight commonly spans 450 gsm to 2000 gsm. A thicker wall does not automatically mean more strength — two boards at the same 4 mm caliper can differ by 400 gsm depending on wall density, and the heavier board carries meaningfully more load.

  • 2–3 mm / 450–650 gsm: light dividers, layer pads, signage, low-weight returnable totes.
  • 4 mm / 700–1000 gsm: the workhorse range for reusable boxes and tote sleeves.
  • 5–6 mm / 1200–2000 gsm: heavy-duty bins, bulk containers, and high-cycle closed-loop assets.

Matching Gauge to Load

Start from the loaded weight and the stacking requirement, not from a board you already have on the shelf. A box that holds 15 kg but sits three-high in a rack needs enough compression strength to carry two boxes above it plus a safety factor. For most B2B reusable programs, we recommend designing to at least 1.6x the maximum static load so the board tolerates handling abuse and humidity without creep. When in doubt, request sample panels in two adjacent gauges and crush-test them against your real product, not a theoretical spec sheet.

Wall Construction and Print

Twin-wall PP uses two flat liners bonded by vertical ribs. Rib pitch affects both stiffness and printability — tighter ribs give a smoother face for flexo or screen print, while wider ribs save weight. If your package carries a branded or regulatory print, confirm the liner is corona-treated so ink adheres through the wash cycles a reusable asset will see. For folding cartons and die-cut blanks, specify the score direction relative to the ribs; scoring across the ribs produces a cleaner, lower-stress fold.

Buyer Checklist

  • Define loaded weight and stack height before choosing a gauge.
  • Request basis weight, not just caliper, on every quote.
  • Design to 1.6x static load for closed-loop durability.
  • Confirm corona treatment if printing.
  • Collect FDA resin documentation for food applications.
