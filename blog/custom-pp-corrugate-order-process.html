The Custom PP Corrugate Order Process: From Spec to Shipment

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • October 2026

Why the Custom Order Process Matters

For procurement and packaging engineers, sourcing custom plastic corrugate is rarely as simple as picking a part number off a catalog page. Cut-to-size polypropylene corrugate is specified around the exact dimensions of your product, the handling environment, and the throughput of your line. Getting the order process right the first time prevents costly re-tooling, protects your production schedule, and ensures the finished packaging performs trip after trip. This guide walks through each stage so your team can move from concept to delivered pallets with confidence.

Step 1: Define the Application

Before requesting a quote, document how the corrugate will be used. Is it a protective divider, a returnable tote, a layer pad, or a die-cut insert? Capture the product weight it must support, the stacking height in your warehouse, exposure to moisture or cold, and whether the part will be washed and reused. These answers drive material gauge, flute direction, and wall thickness. The more precisely you describe the application, the faster a manufacturer can recommend a construction that balances durability against cost.

Step 2: Nail Down the Specifications

Custom corrugate is defined by measurable specs. Typical variables include sheet thickness (commonly 2mm to 5mm), weight per square meter (300 to 1200 gsm), fluted or solid construction, and color or UV additives. For boxes and totes, you will also specify internal dimensions, scoring and fold patterns, and any hand holes or ventilation. When you work with a direct manufacturer such as the team behind custom corrugated plastic sheets from Elipacko, these parameters are confirmed on a drawing before any tooling is cut, which removes ambiguity from the quote.

Step 3: Request Samples and Prototypes

Reputable suppliers provide material swatches and, for die-cut parts, a prototype or first-article sample. This is the stage to validate fit on your actual product, test fold integrity, and confirm the part survives your wash cycle if applicable. Treat samples as a milestone gate: do not release a full production order until a physical part has passed a real-world handling test on your floor. A short delay here saves weeks of rework later.

Step 4: Understand Tooling, MOQs, and Lead Times

Die-cut and custom-folded parts often require tooling, which carries a one-time cost amortized across the order. Minimum order quantities for custom corrugate are typically lower than injection-molded alternatives, making PP corrugate attractive for mid-volume programs. Lead times vary with complexity, but plan for several weeks from approved drawing to first shipment. Building this into your procurement calendar avoids line-down emergencies. Ordering in annual blanket quantities with scheduled releases can further reduce per-unit cost and lock in capacity.

Step 5: Confirm Compliance and Sourcing

If your corrugate contacts food, request documentation that the resin is FDA-compliant. Also confirm country of origin for duty purposes. Elipacko manufactures in Thailand and Vietnam, which carry a 0% antidumping duty rate into the US market, with US manufacturing currently in development. For food and agriculture programs, pairing custom corrugate with other items from the same supplier, such as custom PP packaging boxes, simplifies vendor management and consolidates freight.

Step 6: Place the Order and Plan Reorders

Once the sample is approved and terms are agreed, issue the purchase order referencing the approved drawing revision. Keep a copy of the drawing, the material spec sheet, and the compliance documentation in your supplier file. For reusable programs, track trips per unit and schedule reorders based on attrition rather than waiting for stockouts. A direct relationship with the manufacturer makes revisions, repeat orders, and volume adjustments straightforward.

Takeaways for Procurement Teams

The custom plastic corrugate order process rewards upfront rigor. Define the application, document specs on a drawing, validate with samples, and plan for tooling and lead time. Confirm resin compliance and sourcing to protect both food safety and landed cost. By working directly with a manufacturer rather than a broker, your team gains faster revisions, lower per-unit pricing, and a single point of accountability from spec to shipment.
