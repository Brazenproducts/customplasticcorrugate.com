Cost-Per-Trip Analysis: Custom Plastic Corrugate vs. Single-Use Cardboard

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • September 2026

The Case for Running the Numbers on Packaging Materials

Procurement teams under margin pressure often default to cardboard corrugated because the unit cost looks favorable. A standard single-use cardboard box might cost $2.50–$4.00. A custom plastic corrugate box with the same footprint might run $8.00–$18.00 depending on gauge and complexity. On paper, cardboard wins. But cost-per-unit is the wrong metric for any packaging that travels in a closed loop or semi-closed distribution network. Cost-per-trip — what you actually pay each time a box completes one handling cycle — is the number that matters.

Building a Cost-Per-Trip Model

The formula is straightforward:

Cost per trip = (Unit cost + Lifecycle overhead) ÷ Number of usable trips

For single-use cardboard, cost per trip equals unit cost — the box is consumed on the first cycle. For custom corrugated plastic sheets from Elipacko formed into boxes or trays, the unit cost is amortized across all usable trips, often 50–200 or more cycles depending on the application and handling environment.

Consider a realistic example:

  • Cardboard box: $3.50 unit cost × 1 trip = $3.50 per trip
  • Custom PP corrugate box (4mm): $14.00 unit cost ÷ 80 trips = $0.175 per trip
  • Custom PP corrugate box (4mm) at 40 trips: $14.00 ÷ 40 = $0.35 per trip — still 90% below cardboard

At 80 trips, the PP corrugate box delivers a 95% reduction in per-trip cost. This math is the core driver behind why every major auto manufacturer, pharmaceutical distributor, and food processor running a pool of returnable packaging chooses reusable corrugated plastic over cardboard for closed-loop supply chains.

What to Include in Lifecycle Overhead

A rigorous cost-per-trip model should include overhead costs that cardboard avoids but PP corrugate absorbs efficiently:

  • Washing and sanitation: PP corrugated boxes can be pressure-washed, steam-cleaned, or run through industrial tunnel washers. Budget $0.20–$0.60 per wash cycle for commercial-scale operations. At $0.40 per cycle over 80 washes, that adds $32.00 in washing cost — still substantially below 80 cardboard boxes at $3.50 each ($280).
  • Inspection and retirement: Expect to retire 5–10% of boxes per year due to damage, cracking, or loss. Factor this into your fleet replacement budget on an annualized basis.
  • Return logistics: For true closed-loop operations, inbound freight costs for empty box returns must be allocated. For on-site or semi-closed loops, this cost is minimal or absent.
  • Storage footprint: Empty PP corrugate boxes nest or stack flat. The storage cost premium versus cardboard is typically negligible in organized facilities.

Where Custom Sizing Pays Off Most

Standard-size corrugated plastic boxes introduce hidden costs: wasted void fill, over-packing to prevent product movement, and dimensional weight charges on outbound freight. Custom PP packaging boxes sized precisely to your SKU dimensions eliminate all three. For high-velocity distribution programs, freight savings from dimensional optimization alone can justify the custom tooling investment within a single shipping season.

Break-Even Analysis: When to Make the Switch

The break-even point for switching from single-use cardboard to custom plastic corrugate depends on your annual volume, box cost, and expected trip count. As a rough benchmark:

  • If your annualized box spend exceeds $20,000 and you can recover at least 60% of boxes after each cycle, you almost certainly break even within the first year of the reusable program.
  • At recovery rates above 75%, break-even often occurs within 6–9 months for medium-volume programs.
  • For pharmaceutical, automotive, and food-processing closed-loop circuits where 90% or greater recovery is standard, break-even typically happens within weeks to a few months of launch.

Calculating Your Program ROI

To calculate full program ROI, compare your current annual cardboard spend against the annualized cost of a reusable fleet:

  • Annual cardboard cost: (Units shipped per year) × (Cardboard box unit cost)
  • Annual reusable fleet cost: (Fleet size × PP corrugate unit cost ÷ Expected life in years) + Annual washing and overhead
  • Annual savings: Annual cardboard cost minus Annual reusable fleet cost
  • ROI: Annual savings ÷ Initial fleet investment × 100

For programs with consistent volume and 70% or better box recovery, ROI in years 2 and beyond routinely exceeds 200–400%. The cost-per-trip model consistently favors reusable plastic corrugate once the loop is even partially closed. Procurement teams that have run the analysis rarely return to single-use cardboard for qualifying applications.
