Custom PP Corrugate Spec Guide: Gauge, Wall Thickness, and Load Ratings

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • September 2026

Why Custom Specifications Matter in PP Corrugated Packaging

Off-the-shelf corrugated plastic sheets solve a lot of problems. But when you are running a specialized operation — whether precision parts protection, food-grade bulk containment, or automated line feeding — generic dimensions and thicknesses often introduce their own inefficiencies. Specifying a custom PP corrugated product to your exact requirements eliminates trim waste, reduces nested air gaps, and can meaningfully improve stack stability and load performance across your entire packaging program.

This guide covers the core specification variables procurement teams need to understand when ordering custom corrugated plastic sheets from Elipacko: gauge, wall thickness, flute geometry, and static and dynamic load ratings.

Gauge and Wall Thickness: What the Numbers Mean

Polypropylene corrugated sheet gauge is measured as overall panel thickness — the distance from the outer face to the inner face, spanning the fluted core. Common commercial gauges run from 2mm through 10mm. For most packaging and protection applications, the most-requested gauges are:

  • 2mm–3mm: Light-duty layer pads, slip sheets, and signage substrates. Suitable for unit loads up to roughly 15 lbs static per square foot.
  • 4mm–5mm: The workhorse range. Standard reusable box construction, divider boards, and parts trays. Handles static loads of 25–40 lbs/sq ft depending on flute density.
  • 6mm–8mm: Heavy-duty boxes, bulk bin liners, and floor protection panels. Appropriate for stacked bulk applications with loads reaching 60–80 lbs/sq ft.
  • 10mm: Structural panel applications and heavy corrugated bulk containers. Often reinforced with additional ribbing for load-bearing use.

Wall thickness refers to the face sheet layers on each side of the fluted core. Thicker face sheets resist puncture, abrasion, and delamination. For food and pharma environments where surfaces contact product directly, Elipacko's FDA-compliant virgin PP resin is specified by default at no additional cost premium.

Flute Geometry and Directional Strength

Unlike cardboard corrugated, PP corrugated has a fixed flute direction — the internal flutes run parallel to one edge of the sheet. This creates a material significantly stronger across the flute direction than along it. For procurement teams specifying boxes, trays, or layer pads, this distinction matters considerably in real-world performance:

  • Machine direction (MD) strength: Compression resistance runs perpendicular to the flutes. Boxes should be designed with flutes oriented vertically to maximize column stacking strength under load.
  • Cross direction (CD) flexibility: Sheets bend more easily along the flute direction, which aids folded box construction but limits load-bearing capacity in that axis.
  • Die-cut registration: Custom die-cut blanks must account for flute orientation to achieve consistent crease performance and folded box squareness during assembly at scale.

Load Rating Standards for Custom PP Corrugate Applications

Procurement specifications should clearly state the intended load scenario — static, dynamic, or racked — because each imposes a different stress mode on the sheet:

  • Static load: Weight applied uniformly and held for extended periods. PP corrugated creep under sustained load must be factored into pallet layer pad sizing and stack height limits.
  • Dynamic load: Impact loads from product dropping, vibration in transit, or mechanical handling cycles. Higher-gauge sheets with denser flutes perform better in dynamic environments.
  • Racked or cantilevered load: Relevant for custom shelf liners and divider panels where the sheet spans an unsupported gap. Typically requires 6mm or greater with MD orientation aligned to the span axis.

Ordering Checklist for Custom PP Corrugated Specs

Before requesting a quote, gather the following from your engineering or operations team:

  • Overall panel dimensions (L × W) in inches or mm
  • Required gauge (mm) and acceptable tolerance range
  • Flute direction preference (parallel to length or parallel to width)
  • Face sheet color (natural, black, or custom color match)
  • Edge treatment: raw cut, sealed edge, or punched and die-cut
  • Food contact requirement (FDA-grade virgin PP vs. recycled-content PP)
  • Static load target per square foot and expected trip cycle count
  • Annual volume and delivery frequency for container pricing optimization
