FDA and USDA Compliance for Custom Plastic Corrugate in Food Packaging

Customplasticcorrugate Editorial • October 2026

Food Contact Compliance Starts With the Resin

For food and beverage procurement teams, custom plastic corrugate has to clear a bar that general industrial packaging never faces: direct and indirect food contact compliance. The United States regulates food-contact materials through the FDA, and in meat and poultry operations the USDA layers additional facility and sanitation requirements on top. Specifying corrugate for these environments means thinking about compliance from the resin pellet forward, not as an afterthought bolted on at the end of a buying decision.

The foundation is the base polypropylene resin. FDA-compliant corrugate must be manufactured from resin that meets the requirements of 21 CFR for food-contact substances. Virgin, food-grade PP resin carries the documentation chain that auditors expect to see. Recycled or commodity-grade resin, while cheaper, often cannot provide that lineage and should be avoided for any application touching food.

What FDA Compliance Actually Requires

Buyers sometimes assume that "plastic" is automatically food-safe. It is not. FDA compliance for a food-contact material rests on several pillars that your supplier should be able to document:

  • Approved resin: Base polymer and any additives must be cleared for the intended food-contact use.
  • Controlled additives: Colorants, UV stabilizers, and processing aids must themselves be food-contact approved.
  • Migration limits: The finished material must not transfer substances into food above regulated thresholds.
  • Documentation: A letter of compliance or declaration of conformity tying the finished product to the regulation.

USDA Facility Considerations

In USDA-inspected meat and poultry plants, packaging compliance extends beyond the material to how it behaves in a sanitation program. Surfaces must be cleanable, non-absorbent, and resistant to the aggressive detergents and sanitizers used in daily washdown. Polypropylene corrugate performs well here because its non-porous surface does not harbor moisture or bacteria the way waxed fiberboard does, and it tolerates repeated chemical cleaning without degrading.

For reusable formats, this is a major advantage. A corrugate container that can be washed, sanitized, and returned to service supports the closed-loop hygiene expectations of a USDA facility far better than disposable packaging that is discarded and replaced. Custom-designed boxes can also incorporate drainage features and smooth radii that eliminate water-trapping crevices.

Designing for Cleanability

Compliance is easier to maintain when the physical design cooperates with your sanitation SOP. Smooth welded corners, the absence of absorbent liners, and open structures that drain and dry quickly all reduce contamination risk. Pairing custom corrugate with custom PP packaging boxes engineered for washdown lets you standardize a single cleanable format across multiple product lines.

Document your cleaning chemistry and temperatures when you request samples. PP tolerates a wide range of sanitizers, but confirming compatibility with your specific quaternary or chlorine-based program avoids surprises during validation.

Building Your Compliance File

Auditors want a paper trail. Before you commit to a custom corrugate program, assemble the supplier's declaration of conformity, resin food-contact documentation, and any migration test data. Keep these on file alongside your cleaning validation records so that any USDA or customer audit can be satisfied quickly.

Bottom Line

Food-contact compliance for custom plastic corrugate is a chain that runs from resin selection through additive control, migration performance, and sanitation design. Specify FDA-compliant food-grade PP, confirm USDA cleanability requirements, and collect your documentation up front. Done right, reusable corrugate becomes both a compliance asset and a cost advantage in food operations.
